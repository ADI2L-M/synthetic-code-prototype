**Fully supported** means the claim follows from the current code at its stated scope. **Partially supported** means the code or historical results support a narrower statement. **Unsupported** means the proposed statement cannot be established by the available evidence. Section 5 is for the conceptual mechanism and propositions; Section 6 is for the system artefacts and configured implementation; Section 7 is for operational results and their limits.

## Required wording corrections

1. **Name the comparison target consistently.** Use **Context-Aware Target Profile** for the fixed authentic-data-derived prevalence vector. Replace “threshold profile,” “defect threshold profile,” or an ambiguous “target characteristics” when the statistical target is meant. Use **Observed Synthetic Defect Profile** for detector prevalences among functionally passing generated submissions. The A3 text on page 4 switches to “threshold profile”; this should be harmonized ([A3 conceptual artefact submission, pages 4–5](../research-notes/research-literatures/A3-conceptualize-artefacts.pdf)).
2. **Separate a defect from its category.** In equations, $d$ indexes an individual defect identifier. “Task-independent” and “task-dependent” are grouping labels. MAE divides by the count of target-profile defect identifiers, not by the two categories ([analysis.py, lines 71–75](../services/generation/analysis.py#L71); [experiment.py, lines 142–169](../services/generation/experiment.py#L142)).
3. **Specify comparison and stopping conceptually.** Name equal-weight MAE as a **summary measure**, then state the actual per-defect, uncertainty-adjusted tolerance rule and all-defects stopping predicate. Do not present a fixed absolute tolerance $\tau_d$ or an MAE-only stopping threshold as the implementation ([analysis.py, lines 89–108](../services/generation/analysis.py#L89); [types.py, lines 95–97](../models/types.py#L95)).
4. **Define task independence by applicability, with empirical variation left open.** A task-independent label means the characteristic can be meaningfully assessed across multiple task contexts; it does **not** assert constant or statistically indistinguishable prevalence across those tasks. A task-dependent defect has a structural opportunity tied to particular task constructs. The A3 page-3 wording “occurrence remains comparatively consistent” is a conceptual proposition to test, not an established classification criterion in this prototype ([A3 conceptual artefact submission, page 3](../research-notes/research-literatures/A3-conceptualize-artefacts.pdf); [configuration.py, lines 67–83](../services/generation/configuration.py#L67); [authentic-profile audit, lines 96–102](authentic_student_profile_audit.md#L96)).
5. **Define Generation Specification precisely.** Conceptually, it combines a stable task contract with adaptive defect guidance. In the prototype, a task-level specification is recorded, but the initial guided model request uses a **per-submission brief** containing selected styles, examples, avoidance guidance, and calibration text. Repairs use a distinct revision brief ([workflow.py, lines 245–249 and 285–362](../services/generation/workflow.py#L245); [prompt_builder.py, lines 101–245](../services/generation/prompt_builder.py#L101)).
6. **Use “functional validity under configured tests.”** Do not imply formal proof or that every retained submission is functionally valid. Only PASS outputs enter the observed profile, while failed outputs can remain in batch records after repair attempts ([validator.py, lines 59–109](../services/generation/validator.py#L59); [workflow.py, lines 443–485](../services/generation/workflow.py#L443)).
7. **Call feedback qualitative.** “Increase,” “Reduce,” and “Maintain” become prompt strings and priority ordering. They do not numerically update defect probabilities. State that a discrepancy does not guarantee the next observed prevalence changes in the intended direction ([calibrator.py, lines 4–12](../services/generation/calibrator.py#L4); [assignment.py, lines 52–65](../services/generation/assignment.py#L52)).
8. **Treat representational correspondence as bounded and measurable.** In this prototype it means correspondence on a selected, detector-operationalized defect vector among functionally passing submissions. MAE and per-defect margins are partial indicators; they do not prove that whole submissions or all aspects of novice coding are authentic ([research context, lines 194–233](../research-notes/research-context.md#L194); [analysis.py, lines 8–18 and 55–125](../services/generation/analysis.py#L8)).
9. **Move outcome claims to Section 7.** Section 5 can state a testable expectation that feedback may improve correspondence; Section 7 must state the mixed historical results, baseline choice, repetition count, and limits. Reaching the maximum iteration count is not successful convergence ([experiment.py, lines 428–432](../services/generation/experiment.py#L428); [evaluation table, lines 617–647](../research-notes/design-artefact-evaluation/section-3-design-artefact-evaluation.md#L617)).

## Publication-ready mathematical definitions

Let $t$ index task context, $D_t$ the defect identifiers in its supplied target profile, $k$ the batch-level calibration iteration, $V_{t,k}$ the submissions passing configured functional tests, and $x_{t,d,k}$ the number of these submissions in which detector $d$ reports a defect. These equations are **exact implementation** for the current prototype:

$$
O_{t,d,k}=
\begin{cases}
x_{t,d,k}/|V_{t,k}|,& |V_{t,k}|>0,\\
0,& |V_{t,k}|=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad e_{t,d,k}=T_{t,d}-O_{t,d,k}.
$$

$$
\operatorname{MAE}_{t,k}=
\begin{cases}
|D_t|^{-1}\displaystyle\sum_{d\in D_t}|e_{t,d,k}|,& |D_t|>0,\\
0,& |D_t|=0.
\end{cases}
$$

The configured relative tolerance $\tau$ is common to all defects, but the decision margin $m_{t,d,k}$ is defect- and iteration-specific. For $n=|V_{t,k}|>0$, let $[L_{t,d,k},U_{t,d,k}]$ be the Wilson interval for $x_{t,d,k}/n$ using $z=1.96$, and let $s^T_{t,d}$ be the available empirical target standard error (zero if absent). The implemented rule is:

$$
m_{t,d,k}=
\begin{cases}
\max\!\left(
|T_{t,d}|\tau,\;
z\sqrt{(s^T_{t,d})^2+
\left(\frac{U_{t,d,k}-L_{t,d,k}}{2z}\right)^2}
\right),&n>0,\\
|T_{t,d}|\tau,&n=0.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
a_{t,d,k}=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{Increase},&e_{t,d,k}>m_{t,d,k},\\
\mathrm{Reduce},&e_{t,d,k}<-m_{t,d,k},\\
\mathrm{Maintain},&|e_{t,d,k}|\leq m_{t,d,k},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{profileAccepted}_{t,k}
=\bigwedge_{d\in D_t}\left(|e_{t,d,k}|\leq m_{t,d,k}\right).
$$

These correspond to [analysis.py, lines 35–125](../services/generation/analysis.py#L35), [experiment.py, lines 142–169](../services/generation/experiment.py#L142), and [types.py, lines 95–97](../models/types.py#L95). The rule-based next-constraint mapping is **exact implementation**, not a proportional controller: $C_{t,d,k+1}=f(a_{t,d,k})$, where $f$ maps Increase, Reduce, and Maintain to the respective literal instructions “strengthen,” “reduce,” and “maintain the generation constraint” ([calibrator.py, lines 4–12](../services/generation/calibrator.py#L4)). The proposition that this update causes convergence is **conceptual and testable**, not an equation implemented by the controller.

## Revised Section 5.4: Iteratively Calibrated Synthetic Generation Framework

The third conceptual artefact specifies how empirical observations can guide and evaluate synthetic novice-code generation. It builds on the Context-Aware Synthetic Representation Framework by treating the **Context-Aware Target Profile** as a fixed representation of selected, observable defects in authentic functionally valid submissions. Under Representation Theory, the value of a synthetic dataset depends on how faithfully its measured characteristics correspond to those of the real-world domain. This makes distributional correspondence, rather than the apparent plausibility of isolated generated programs, the design target. The framework does not assume that a task-independent defect has constant prevalence across tasks: the term indicates broad contextual applicability, while task-dependent defects require particular structural opportunities.

For a selected programming task, the framework translates the target profile into a Generation Specification. This combines an unchanged functional task contract with defect-oriented guidance for applicable characteristics. Target prevalences inform how often characteristics are requested across a batch; the specification directs generation without requiring each individual program to reproduce the entire distribution. In the prototype, an initial per-submission brief names selected styles, functional requirements, and examples. Later calibration can alter the defect guidance, while the task contract and empirical target remain fixed. This distinction separates the conceptual target from the adjustable means of producing synthetic code.

Generated submissions undergo functional validation against the configured task tests. Only those that pass contribute to the **Observed Synthetic Defect Profile**, calculated from detected defect frequencies among passing submissions. Functional validity and representational correspondence are therefore assessed separately. For each target-profile defect, a signed target-minus-observed difference identifies whether the characteristic is less or more prevalent than intended. Mean absolute error summarizes the absolute differences across the included defect identifiers with equal weight. The prototype additionally compares each difference with a target-relative margin enlarged when sampling uncertainty warrants it; a small numerical mismatch need not trigger adjustment.

When a difference exceeds its decision margin, the framework translates its direction into an Increase or Reduce action; otherwise it Maintains the guidance. In the prototype these actions become qualitative prompt instructions and influence the priority of mandatory category assignments for the next batch. They do not numerically update defect probabilities. A new batch is then generated, validated, detected, and compared with the same target profile. Each batch is one calibration iteration; revisions attempted within a single submission are separate generation attempts. The loop stops when all target-profile defects fall within their decision margins or the configured iteration limit is reached. A limit stop does not indicate convergence. The prototype may retain the best eligible nonconverged batch subject to a quality non-regression rule; profile acceptance itself is not a proof that every output is functionally correct beyond the configured tests.

The framework consequently makes representational correspondence an observable, revisable objective while keeping its empirical reference stable. Its proposed benefit is testable against a fixed-prompt baseline through defect-profile error, tolerance satisfaction, functional pass rate, and category coverage. Improvement is an empirical question: calibration may leave alignment unchanged or worsen it for a given task.

## Testable propositions prompted by A3 feedback

These belong in Section 5 as **propositions for evaluation**, not findings:

1. **P1, contextual variation:** Across a prespecified set of eligible tasks, task-dependent defects show greater between-task variation in prevalence than task-independent defects. State the dispersion statistic and eligible-task rule before testing; the current researcher-defined labels do not themselves prove P1 ([eligibility.py, lines 17–46](../services/research/eligibility.py#L17); [authentic-profile audit, lines 96–102](authentic_student_profile_audit.md#L96)).
2. **P2, distributional alignment:** Under matched task, model, sample size, and generation budget, iterative calibration yields lower selected-batch MAE and/or a higher proportion of target defects within their decision margins than fixed task-aware prompting. Report both measures and all repetitions. The current T2 historical result runs counter to an unconditional version ([evaluation table, lines 627–647](../research-notes/design-artefact-evaluation/section-3-design-artefact-evaluation.md#L627)).
3. **P3, functional-quality bound:** Relative to a prespecified fixed-prompt comparator, adding defect guidance and calibration does not lower functional pass rate by more than a preregistered margin $\delta$. The existing implementation has no such numerical threshold; this is an evaluation criterion to set prospectively ([calibrator.py, lines 15–37](../services/generation/calibrator.py#L15); [experiment.py, lines 400–426](../services/generation/experiment.py#L400)).
4. **P4, correspondence scope:** Changes in targeted defect-profile measures need not imply broader novice-code realism. If broader correspondence is claimed, evaluate additional independently defined properties or expert ratings. This keeps the representation claim tied to measured phenomena ([research context, lines 194–233](../research-notes/research-context.md#L194)).

## Outstanding empirical and technical limitations

- **Historical, narrow evaluation.** The available three-repetition suite uses one recorded model, batch size 20, and three task families. Its logs are schema 2, whereas the current writer declares schema 3 and adds provenance fields. No current-schema experiment result establishes present-loop effectiveness ([experiment.py, lines 277–309](../services/generation/experiment.py#L277); [evaluation basis, lines 617–625](../research-notes/design-artefact-evaluation/section-3-design-artefact-evaluation.md#L617)).
- **Detector and target validity.** Alignment is only as credible as detector accuracy, authentic-profile estimation, and task applicability decisions. Runtime generation does not revalidate arbitrary target keys against the research eligibility matrix ([configuration.py, lines 76–83](../services/generation/configuration.py#L76); [workflow.py, lines 252–270](../services/generation/workflow.py#L252); [authentic-profile audit](authentic_student_profile_audit.md)).
- **Finite functional gate.** Configured examples and a timed subprocess establish test-case PASS, not correctness for all valid inputs. No minimum valid batch size gates profile acceptance, and a batch with no passing submissions receives zero observed prevalence ([validator.py, lines 59–109](../services/generation/validator.py#L59); [analysis.py, lines 8–18 and 89–108](../services/generation/analysis.py#L8)).
- **No guaranteed corrective direction.** The action strings contain no numerical gain or dose; random-seed changes, mandatory overlays, repair, and interacting defect styles can change multiple observed frequencies. The planner has no general incompatibility matrix ([assignment.py, lines 70–115](../services/generation/assignment.py#L70); [workflow.py, lines 183–218](../services/generation/workflow.py#L183)).
- **Selection versus causal effect.** A lower *selected* MAE can result from best-iteration selection and repeated draws. Report both initial-to-final within-run trajectories and selected-condition comparisons. The T1 latest-suite iterative records selected iteration 1 in all repetitions, so their slight condition-summary advantage does not show successful corrective feedback ([T1 record](../research-notes/synthetic-generation-experiments/experiment-log-300926161821.jsonl#L1)).
- **Scope of authenticity.** Per-defect prevalence and MAE measure selected marginal defect frequencies, not joint defect patterns, semantic strategy, or individual plausibility. Functional and category metrics should accompany alignment claims ([analytics.py, lines 66–103 and 149–172](../services/generation/analytics.py#L66)).
